Introduction

In this lesson, you'll add a Markdown text panel to provide context for your dashboard and learn how to arrange and resize panels. Well-organized dashboards with clear descriptions are easier for others to understand and use effectively.

Prerequisites: Completed "Adding More Visualizations" challenge with two charts on your dashboard

**Adding Text and Arranging Panels**

1. Once again, navigate to the dashboard you were working on in the last lesson. Next, lets try using a different visualization editor. Let's create a text description. Later we can use this in more creative ways. For now we will just include a short description.

Select the `Add panel` button. And select `Markdown Text` option in the sidepanel.

<img width="888" height="481" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/6f36f5f8-0f91-41b9-b970-b8d9560587fa" />

🔍 Why this matters

Markdown panels add context that helps dashboard viewers understand what they're looking at. Use them for titles, descriptions, instructions, or links to related resources. A dashboard with clear explanations is more useful to colleagues who didn't build it.

2. In the editor, type a short description, such as:

```bash
# Logs Overview
This is the Logs Overview **Dashboard**
```

You can use Markdown syntax to change the formatting of your text. See some examples below. Don't forget to click `Update` before you `Save and return`.

<img width="1728" height="594" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/97e1ce92-cd8f-46fe-9d7e-05ab8c5d1f49" />

💡 Best practice

Common Markdown formatting includes: `# Header` for titles, **bold** for emphasis, - item for bullet lists, and [link text](URL) for hyperlinks. You can also include images and tables. Keep descriptions concise—viewers come to dashboards for visualizations, not lengthy text.

3. You can rearrange your panels by selecting the "move"-icon on the top left of the panel — again when you hover your mouse over the panel, this icon will appear.

<img width="836" height="614" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2d668713-cdbf-4007-919b-6382f33f87a3" />

4. Now let's make your dashboard look nice. You can easily resize your panels by clicking the bottom right corner and dragging it — notice that when you hover your mouse over the bottom right corner, the color of the corner changes slightly and your cursor shape changes.

<img width="1031" height="431" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e0ee6fcf-908d-4cd8-a721-9c0de30f7194" />

💡 Best practice

Place the most important visualizations at the top-left, as that's where viewers look first. Group related panels together. Size panels according to their importance and detail level—summary metrics can be smaller, while detailed charts need more space to be readable.

5. You can click on the gear icon in the top right of the visualization to open a flyout from the right that allows you to change the title and description of your panel. Click Save to save your changes.

<img width="1052" height="433" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2ca3df66-5afd-4966-92fc-3cb28cb0c15b" />

🔍 Why this matters

Descriptive panel titles help viewers quickly understand each visualization's purpose. The default title shows the visualization type or field name, which may not be meaningful to others. Custom titles like "Daily Request Volume" or "Visitor Operating Systems" communicate intent clearly.
